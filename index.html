Frame Anatomy

What do all these terms mean?

New to frame making? Tap any term to learn exactly what it is and why it matters for your build.

Key terms

Face Width
How wide the frame moulding looks from the front — from the outer edge to the inner edge. A wider face width means a bigger, bolder frame border.
Frame Opening
The visible rectangular hole in the front of the frame. The glazing and artwork sit behind this opening. Every other dimension is calculated from this.
Rabbet Overlap
The lip of the frame that hangs over the edge of the glazing, holding everything in place. Standard is 1/4". This determines how much bigger the frame opening is than the artwork.
Rabbet Depth
How deep the L-shaped groove is routed into the back inner edge of the moulding. Must be deep enough to hold your full stack: glazing + art + backing board.
Stock Thickness
The front-to-back depth of your raw lumber before any cuts. After routing the rabbet, the remaining wood becomes the front face of your frame.
Artwork Size (your input)
For this calculator, enter the full size of your artwork including any attached mat. If your mat is already mounted, measure the outside edge of the mat.

The rabbet — what you're routing

Looking at the end grain of your moulding stock, this is the L-shaped groove you'll route into the inner back corner. The rabbet creates a ledge that holds all your materials and a lip that keeps them in from the front.

What sits in the rabbet (front to back):

Glazing (acrylic or glass) Typical sheet: 1/8"
Art package Mat board + artwork + mounting board — measure yours
Backing board Hardboard or foam board: typically 1/8"

Total stack thickness must be ≤ your rabbet depth. The calculator checks this for you.

Glazing is cut 1/16" smaller than the frame opening on each side (1/8" total) to allow for thermal movement and handling clearance — the calculator accounts for this automatically.

How the calculator works

Every number the calculator gives you traces to one of four formulas. Understanding them makes you a better builder.

① Frame Opening
Opening = Artwork + (2 × Overlap)

The rabbet slot is made slightly larger than your mat so the overlap (lip) can grip its edge. The visible window you see through the front equals your original artwork size.

② Glazing Size
Glazing = Opening − (2 × 1/16″)

The 1/16″ relief per side (1/8″ total) allows for thermal movement and handling clearance. Applies to both acrylic and glass.

③ Cut Length — each piece
Cut = Opening + (2 × Face Width)

Measured long-point to long-point for 45° miter joints. Top and bottom pieces use the width opening; left and right pieces use the height opening.

④ Total Moulding Needed
(2 × top + 2 × side) × 1.25

The 25% waste factor covers tearout, mis-cuts, and end-grain loss at each miter — standard allowance for 45° miter work. Reduce to 15% for long, straight stock with no figure.
