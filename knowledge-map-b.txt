Getting picture frame measurements wrong is the most common — and most expensive — DIY framing mistake. Cut the glass a hair too wide and it cracks. Order the mat too small and it covers your artwork. This calculator gives you every dimension you need in one step: frame opening, mat opening, glass cut size, and outer frame size.

To measure a picture frame correctly, you need four numbers: your artwork size, the mat border width, the rabbet overlap, and the glass clearance. Each dimension in the frame stack is derived from the artwork size outward — not guessed from the frame’s outer edge inward.

Quick Answer: How to measure a picture frame
Measure your artwork width and height. The frame opening equals artwork size plus twice the rabbet overlap (typically 0.25 in per side). The mat opening equals artwork size minus twice the mat overlap (typically 0.25 in per side). Glass cuts to the frame opening minus 1/16 in per side. Use the calculator below for instant results.
TL;DR – Quick Summary
Frame opening = artwork size + (2 × rabbet overlap), typically +0.5 in per dimension.
Mat opening = artwork size − (2 × mat overlap), typically −0.5 in per dimension.
Glass cuts 1/8 in smaller than the frame opening (1/16 in clearance per side).
Rabbet overlap is the frame lip that hides the glass edge — standard is 0.25 in.
Outer frame size = frame opening + (2 × moulding width) — varies by frame profile.
Always measure artwork first; never measure from the frame’s outer edge inward.
Dimension	Typical Value / Rule	Purpose
Rabbet overlap	0.25 in (1/4 in)	Frame lip that holds glass in place
Glass clearance	1/16 in per side (1/8 in total)	Prevents cracking from thermal expansion
Mat overlap	0.25 in per side (standard)	Hides photo border, holds artwork
Mat border width	1.5–3 in (decorative)	Visual breathing room around artwork
Rabbet depth	0.5–0.75 in	Holds full stack: glass + mat + art + backing

How to Use This Picture Frame Measurement Calculator
Follow these four steps and you will have every dimension you need before you buy or cut anything.

Measure your artwork — use a ruler or tape measure on the actual artwork, not the packaging. Record width and height in inches.
Enter mat border width — this is the visible mat width you want on each side. A 2-inch border is a common decorative choice. Enter 0 if you are not using a mat.
Enter rabbet overlap — the standard is 0.25 inches. Check your specific frame moulding if you are unsure; measure the lip with calipers.
Enter moulding width — measure the face width of the frame profile from the outer edge to the inner edge. This gives you the outer frame size for ordering custom frames.
Click Calculate — the result card shows all eight dimensions with fractional inch equivalents and a numbered solution you can hand to a glass cutter or framer.
What Is a Frame Opening — and Why Does It Drive Every Other Measurement?
The frame opening is the rectangular hole in the front of the frame through which you see the artwork. Every other dimension in a picture frame is derived from it.

Think of a picture frame as a stack of layers, each sized relative to the one inside it:

The rabbet is the stepped groove on the inner back edge of the moulding. Its lip — the part you see from the front — overlaps the glass edge by the rabbet overlap amount (typically 0.25 in). That overlap is what holds everything in and defines the visible frame opening.
